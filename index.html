This is the v4 design, checked in software only. The CAD passed its clearance checks (no part touches another in 45 poses of the hood and the moving teeth), its ball contact faces match the shooter simulation's, and every print here is the real slicer output for our printers. Nobody has built or tested a v4 turret yet, so the first build is also the test.

Check these on the first build

  1. Nut slots in the ring ears. Drop an M4 nut into each of the ring's 4 ear slots before the ring goes on the 3208 (step 1): it should fall into its hex seat, flats up and down. The slots are new in v4: in the v3 ring code these pockets were sealed inside the print, so check a v3 ring too if one is in use.
  2. Squishy wheel on its shaft. The wheel now sits straight on its REX shaft, driven by the hex bore of the printed hub cast into it, with a collar against each face; there are no printed flanges. The bore size was read from photos of our wheels (about 7.5 mm across the flats, a REX fit), not measured: slide a REX shaft through it before you cut the shaft.
  3. Seat tape and file amounts. How far tape or a file in a seat moves the squeeze (1 mm in a seat moved POLLEN's squeeze about 0.63 mm and NECTAR's about 0.95 mm) comes from v3 and was not re-checked for v4. Measure the squeeze on the first v4 before you rely on those numbers.
  4. Light sanding on the moving teeth. Supports hold the tips' flat lower lane edges in two thin bands (25.2-25.5 mm and 63.6-63.9 mm up the print). The ball bands start 0.2-0.5 mm above them. Sand those two bands lightly and leave the ball faces alone.
  5. Support interface, set by hand. The sliced files print their supports in PETG, which fuses to a PETG part. For the 3 supported prints (hood, moving teeth, ring): in Bambu Studio load PLA (or Bambu Support For PLA/PETG) in the right nozzle, then Process > Support: set the support/raft interface filament to it and the top Z distance to 0, and re-slice before printing.

Before you start (lab checks)

  1. Measure the balls: NECTAR's real diameter (tape round the middle, divided by pi, on a few balls) and a few POLLEN. The hood was built for POLLEN 71.97-73.04 mm and NECTAR 89.86-90.96 mm (calipers that read a little low; the manual says 91.44 mm). NECTAR's real size sets its seat, so write the numbers down before you tune the squeeze.
  2. Cut coupon.dxf in the real 1/4 in acetal first: an F686ZZ must roll in one slot with no rattle (13.0 / 13.1 / 13.2 mm; the plates use 13.1), a 1611 bearing must press in by hand and stay (13.9 / 14.0 / 14.1; the plates use 14.0), an M4 must pass 4.2 / 4.4 (the plates use 4.4). If a different size fits better than the one the plates use, change that size in the plate files before you cut the plates.
  3. Make sure your laser is allowed to cut acetal (it gives off formaldehyde, so it needs extraction).
  4. Print the sector coupon and the 33T angle pinion first (1.3 h together on the H2D): mesh them by hand, no binding, no skipped teeth.
  5. Squishy wheel: a REX shaft must slide through the hex bore of its cast-in hub with no rattle, and the wheel should be 40 mm wide (its 2 collars sit against its faces, step 5).
  6. 3208 gear: are the back-lug and front holes tapped M4? (Only the 4 side ones are needed.)
  7. Servos: program both to their ends and check they turn 300 deg. The angle drive uses 251 deg of it (84 %), leaving 24 deg spare each way when centred; the sector's end blocks sit 4 deg of exit (35 deg of servo) past each end, so the servo stops first. The hood servo uses 116 deg from POLLEN to NECTAR.
  8. Seats and squeeze: after assembly, check the rollers sit hard in both slot ends (POLLEN and NECTAR) with a ball in, then tune each ball's squeeze with tape in, or a file on, its seat ends (Adjusting the squeeze, below).
  9. Belts: both run on fixed centres at their exact lengths (HTD 245 mm at 82.50 mm, GT2 360 mm at 130.78 mm), no tensioner. Press each belt's free span: it should give a little and never jump a tooth when you hold the squishy and turn the flywheel by hand.
  10. Motor face: the v4 motor hangs on its face alone (no motor cup). M4 x 14 button heads: the motor's threaded holes are drilled 10.6 mm deep at full width (goBILDA's STEP), so M4 x 14 puts 7.65 mm of screw in them, 2 mm short of the bottom. Run one screw in by hand first; if it bottoms before the head seats, use M4 x 12.
Changed for the build: the ring's 4 ear nut pockets were sealed inside the print in the first v4 CAD (the nuts could not go in; the v3 ring code had the same pockets). Each now has a drop-in slot from the ear's top (8.4 x 3.2 mm); the new ring lies inside the old one and its contact faces are unchanged. Screw lengths corrected from the CAD's own notes: plates to ring ears M4 x 20, hood servo ears M4 x 16, ring to the 3208 gear 8 screws, motor face M4 x 14, angle pinion to the 1906 hub M4 x 12 button heads with no washer (a washer would touch the moving teeth).
Changed 2026-11-11: the squishy wheel sits straight on its 8 mm REX shaft. Our cast wheels have a printed hub with a hex REX bore, so the hex drives the wheel and 2 goBILDA 2910 collars against its faces locate it. The 2 printed squishy flanges and their 3 x M3 x 50 screws and nylocs are gone.

Adjusting the squeeze

What it was built forThe hood and moving teeth carry the shooter simulation's contact faces exactly. POLLEN: 2.2 mm of squeeze on the 72.5 mm design ball at its tightest point; our 14 measured POLLEN (71.97-73.04 mm) get 2.3-3.75 %. NECTAR: 4.15 mm on a 90.43 mm ball; our 8 measured NECTAR (89.86-90.96 mm, calipers that read a little low) get 2.2-3.6 %. One curve cannot fit both balls exactly over the whole path, so the squeeze varies a little along the hood.
Per ball, at the plates (main adjustment)The round ends of the 2 hook slots in each plate are the hood's seats: the POLLEN end (where the rollers sit with the hood in) and the NECTAR end (the bottom of the drop). The ball pushes the hood into its seat, so tape (or shim stock) in a seat holds the hood closer to the flywheel = more squeeze for that ball; a file does the opposite. Do all 4 slot ends of a seat the same (both plates, both slots). In v3 one mm in the seat moved POLLEN's squeeze 0.63 mm and NECTAR's 0.95 mm; v4's hook has the same shape at its ends but these factors were NOT re-measured for v4. File the POLLEN ends at most 1.2 mm (the toggle has 1.33 mm of spare reach).
Both balls, on the hoodUHMW tape on the ball bands of the hood and moving teeth adds its thickness to both balls' squeeze (and takes the wear). Sanding removes a few tenths. Keep tape edges running along the ball's path, never across it.
Bigger changes: the next hood printRe-print the hood and moving teeth with a different squeeze for either ball (the CAD rebuilds them from the simulation's settings). The ring and the plates stay.
Then make it permanentWrite down the final tape / file amount per seat: the plates can then be re-cut with the seats moved, so the tape comes out.
Squishy feedFixed by design (no slot): its squeeze changes with the wheel itself (a different diameter or softness) or with tape on the ring wall opposite it.

Wear and hardening

  • Small loaded parts print solid (100 %): the angle pinion, the GT2 pulley, both crank arms, the test coupon.
  • The toggle gears are bought steel and brass (no printed toggle gears); the link is laser-cut 1/4 in acetal riding on the aluminium knee tube and the steel pin.
  • Big prints: 0.16 mm layers, 6 walls (2.5 mm of solid skin), 6 top / 5 bottom layers, 40 % gyroid.
  • UHMW tape on the ball bands and on the rails the moving teeth ride on; dry PTFE lube on the T-slots. Replace tape when it wears through.
  • Supports (hood, moving teeth, ring): PETG supports printed onto PETG fuse to the part. Load PLA (or Bambu 'Support For PLA-PETG') in the second (right) nozzle; in Bambu Studio, Process > Support: set the support/raft interface filament to it and the top Z distance to 0, re-slice and print: the supports then lift off clean. (The slicer's command line crashes with any second filament, so the files hold PETG supports.) Drill printed holes to size; sand the T-heads until the teeth slide freely.

Assembly

In each picture the parts of that step are in colour and what is already built is pale grey. Bench steps show the sub-assembly alone. Open any step in 3D to turn it.

Step 1

Nuts into the ring ears, ring onto the turret

  1. Drop an M4 hex nut into each of the 4 slots in the tops of the ring's two side ears: it falls into its hex seat, flats up and down. (These take the plates' screws in steps 8 and 10.)
  2. Stand the ring on the 3208's gear, its ears over the gear's threaded holes at X +-56 mm.
  3. 4 x M4 x 18 socket heads down through the ears' counterbores into the gear's holes 8 mm either side of the axis.
  4. The 2 back lugs and the 2 front holes inside the NECTAR teeth take 2 x M4 x 16 and 2 x M4 x 8 only if those gear holes are tapped (try a screw).

Step 2 · on the bench

Plate A: bearings, motor, hood servo, 24T gear

  1. Press 3 x 1611 bearings into plate A's 14 mm holes from the OUTER face (flywheel, squishy, crank shaft): the flange sits on the outer face.
  2. Motor face on plate A's inner face, shaft out through the 18 mm hole: 4 x M4 x 14 button heads from outside (lab check 10). There is no motor cup in v4: the face carries the motor, so tighten these well.
  3. Hood servo (25-2 Torque) ears on the inner face, body out through the window (it sits tilted 45 deg back): 4 x M4 x 16 button heads from inside + nylocs outside, snug only (the mesh is set in step 9).
  4. Drive the hood servo to the end of its travel you will use as POLLEN (POLLEN to NECTAR is 116 deg of the servo's 300: leave at least 120 deg in the NECTAR direction), then press the brass 24T onto the spline, spline face against the case, and fit its screw.

Step 3 · on the bench

Plate B: bearings

  1. 3 x 1611 bearings into plate B from the OUTER face, flange outside (flywheel, squishy, crank shaft).
  2. Plate B has no motor mount and no windows; its 2 small holes at the bottom front take the angle servo's zip tie (step 12).

Step 4 · on the bench

Flywheel pack

  1. Sonic Hub (1309) on the Hogback's web, inside its rim: 4 x M4 x 10 from the web's far side into the hub's threads.
  2. 3 steel discs each side on the Hogback's 48 mm hole pattern: 8 x M4 x 25 thread-forming socket heads a side.
  3. Slide the 182 mm REX shaft through, centre the pack (92.5 mm from the shaft's B end to the Hogback's middle) and tighten the hub's 2 clamp screws: once the plates are on they cannot be reached.

Step 5 · on the bench

Squishy wheel

  1. Slide the cast squishy wheel onto the 181 mm REX shaft: the printed hub cast into it has a hex bore that fits the shaft, and the hex drives the wheel. No screws, no printed parts.
  2. Centre the wheel 97.4 mm from the shaft's B end (the end that will carry the GT2 pulley).
  3. A 2910 clamping collar against each face of the wheel: push both in until they touch the hub, then tighten their clamp screws. The wheel must not slide along the shaft.
open in 3D
Step 6 · on the bench

Hood: roller nuts, moving teeth, angle drive

  1. Moving teeth first, after printing: sand the two thin bands where supports held the tips' flat lower edges (the layers at 25.2-25.5 mm and 63.6-63.9 mm up the print): the ball bands start 0.2-0.5 mm above them. Sand the 2 T-heads until they slide in the hood's rails.
  2. Slide an M5 nyloc into each of the hood's 4 roller bosses through its side slot (nylon ring toward the middle).
  3. Moving teeth: their T-heads into the 2 rail slots from the FRONT end, swing them back along the rails to mid travel.
  4. Angle servo (25-3 Speed) on the hood's 10 mm fin: 4 x M4 x 20 button heads + nylocs, loose in the 1 mm slots.
  5. Teeth at mid travel (lip 70 deg), servo at the middle of its range (the drive uses 251 of its 300 deg: 24 spare each way); 1906 hub on the spline, then the 33T pinion on the hub's boss: 4 x M4 x 12 button heads, NO washers (a washer touches the moving teeth at the highest exit; the heads clear them by 0.66 mm). They reach 3.6 mm into the hub, 1.8 mm short of the servo case (M4 x 14 would touch it). Push the servo toward the sector with a 0.1 mm paper strip in the mesh, tighten the fin screws.
  6. Turn the servo by hand to both ends: the servo stops first; the sector's end blocks sit 4 deg of exit (35 deg of servo) past each end. Zip-tie the servo lead across the block on the hood's back.

Step 7 · on the bench

Yoke

  1. M5 nyloc into crank arm A's hex pocket (its outer face).
  2. M5 x 90 socket head through arm B, the 74.5 mm aluminium tube (with the acetal link on it) and arm A, into the nyloc: snug, the 3 pieces are now one yoke.
  3. Both arms have hex REX bores: the crank shaft goes through them in step 9, so keep the two bores lined up.

Step 8

Plate A onto the ring, then the flywheel, squishy and hood

  1. Plate A against the ring's A ear: 2 x M4 x 20 socket heads from outside into the 2 nuts you dropped into that ear in step 1.
  2. Flywheel pack: its shaft's A end through plate A's bearing from inside. Squishy: the same through its bearing.
  3. Hood in from the open B side. Add DIN 988 shim rings under the washer until the flange-to-washer gap is the plate + 0.1 mm (this side locates the hood).

Step 9

Yoke in: link to the hood, crank shaft through

  1. Link onto the hood's clevis: ISO 7379 6 x 20 shoulder screw with a DIN 125 6.4 washer under the head, through both lugs and the link, M5 nyloc.
  2. Hood seated at POLLEN (rollers in the slots' POLLEN ends), hood servo at its POLLEN command.
  3. Slide the 157 mm crank shaft in from outside plate A: through plate A's bearing, the spacer stack (the 8 mm steel spacer 1522-0010-0080 + the 0.25 mm shim 2807-0811-0250 = 8.25 mm), the 40T steel gear (hub toward plate A), arm A, then arm B. Turn the yoke until the 40T meshes with the 24T at POLLEN, push the 40T's hub against the spacer stack and tighten its 2 set screws on the shaft's flats.
  4. Set the toggle mesh: loosen the hood servo's 4 ear screws (their 4.4 mm holes give +-0.2 mm), push the servo toward the 40T with a 0.1 mm paper strip in the mesh, tighten, pull the strip out.
  5. End play: the yoke may slide about 0.7 mm between the bearings (0.40 mm on the A side + 0.30 on B). If it is more, add a second 0.25 mm shim; if the yoke binds, take the shim out.

Step 10

Plate B on

  1. Plate B over the 3 shaft ends (flywheel, squishy, and arm B's boss with the crank shaft) into its bearings, against the ring's B ear: 2 x M4 x 20 socket heads into that ear's nuts.
  2. Back tie behind the motor: 120 mm + 12 mm goBILDA REX standoffs joined by an M4 x 8 set screw (132 mm), 2 x M4 x 10 button heads through the plates.
  3. Plate B's 2 rollers: F686ZZ in the slots, 12 mm washers inside the slot, shoulder screws from outside into the captured nuts (this side floats +-0.5 mm).

Step 11

Outside: pulleys, belts, collars

  1. Plate A side: a 3 mm steel spacer (1522-0010-0030) + HTD 16T on the flywheel shaft; the motor's HTD 16T lined up with it by a straightedge; the HTD 245 mm belt (82.5 mm centres: exact, no tensioner).
  2. Plate B side: a 3 mm spacer + the GT2 20T on the flywheel shaft; the printed 76T on the squishy shaft (boss against the bearing), a collar outside it; the GT2 360 mm belt (130.78 mm centres: exact).
  3. Collars: the squishy shaft outside plate A; the crank shaft outside plate B.

Step 12

Cables and the first run

  1. Angle servo lead: from the zip-tie block on the hood's back, a 70 mm loop to the 2 zip-tie holes at plate B's bottom front, then down plate B (never inside |X| < 48: the balls run there).
  2. Hood servo lead along plate A's outside, away from the gears. Motor leads out of its end cap.
  3. Checks: the hood rolls POLLEN <-> NECTAR without a knock (116 deg of the hood servo); the teeth sweep the whole exit range smoothly; spin the flywheel by hand: the squishy turns 3.8 x slower, no belt jumps a tooth; the comb gaps stay open.
  4. Next day: re-tighten every screw that clamps acetal (servo ears, ring ears, motor face): acetal creeps.
